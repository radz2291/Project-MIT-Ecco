## What the shape means

A waveform normally plots a changing quantity against time (or position). Its vertical bends are values, not the physical trajectory of a strand. In a longitudinal sound wave, particle motion runs along the direction of propagation. A bent tube therefore gives the wrong spatial cue for pressure travelling through the fluid. [Evident: wave propagation](https://ims.evidentscientific.com/en/learn/ndt-tutorials/flaw-detection/wave-propagation).

The transmitter's resonance and damping govern how many oscillations remain after excitation: stronger damping generally gives a shorter pulse and broader bandwidth; weak damping gives longer ringing. These are qualitative relationships, not measured ECCO parameters. [Precision Acoustics: transducer FAQ](https://www.acoustics.co.uk/reference/faq/).

The received pulse also depends on the excitation and transducer response, propagation medium and distance, reflecting surface, and receiving electronics. Frequency-dependent attenuation can reduce high frequencies more strongly, changing the shape as well as amplitude. The cited paper concerns high-frequency test systems; its example frequencies are not ECCO specifications. [Evident: transducer application considerations](https://evidentscientific.com/it/applications/resources/white-papers/applications-high-frequency-transducers).

Measured-data animation requires samples, sampling interval or timestamps, amplitude units, trigger definition, sensor/medium context, and gain/filter information. A recorded receiver trace can directly drive a receiver response; it does not uniquely reconstruct pressure everywhere along the path. Any spatial reconstruction needs an explicit propagation model and assumptions.
