## SHOT S02-A — FIRST CONTACT

**Film time:** 0:10–0:13 · **Duration:** 3 seconds
**Primary image:** `S02_A_First_Contact.png`

### Camera composition

An intimate, three-quarter close-up along the ECCO body. The metal extends diagonally through the frame, with a dark rock wall nearby. The composition follows the premium cinematic finishing reference selected for the project: restrained but realistic metallic reflections, tactile dark geology, deep shadows, subtle depth and a narrow, believable sense of downhole space.

The camera is placed *outside* the tool, within an artistic cutaway/annular viewpoint, never literally inside the pipe. Let ECCO's silhouette and a true outward-facing ultrasonic sensor be readable, but allow most of the surroundings to remain hidden. Frame the scene so the small wall-reveal area is visible within the same composition as the originating sensor.

### What happens

We emerge from the darkness of Section 1. ECCO is barely visible.

One pulse fires. A small portion of the rock wall briefly becomes visible. **The echo returns to its originating sensor**, and the illuminated patch fades back toward darkness.

Two more pulses create isolated glimpses of the wall as the tool begins or continues rotating. These glimpses are short, local and clearly separate, rather than lighting the entire borehole.

The pulses are *visual shorthand for inaudible/optically invisible ultrasound*. Light appears only as a cinematic effect to help viewers locate the interaction.

### Camera movement

Almost stationary, with a very subtle forward push. Avoid a dramatic orbit this early; the sound contact, not the camera, should create the tension. The drill string/tool can rotate slowly enough for the audience to read the sensing direction, but the entire assembly turns together.

### Audience experience

*“Something invisible has just been detected.”*

The viewer should feel curiosity and a small reveal, not yet understand the entire imaging system.

### End composition

ECCO remains partially in darkness, but two or three locations on the wall have been briefly revealed. The camera has established a clear visual relationship: **sensor ↔ nearby wall**.

### Timing ideas (director-level beats, not approved frame locks)

- **0:10.0:** Arrival from black, barely legible metal and geology.
- **0:10.7:** One primary pulse and small localized wall flash.
- **0:11.3:** Echo visibly returns; spot quickly dims.
- **0:11.7–0:13.0:** Two abbreviated additional pulse–echo glimpses, setting up the analytic treatment.

### Transition to S02-B

Use the final outgoing/returning echo rhythm as the bridge. Either cut closer to the same visible sensor/rock relation or perform a restrained push that feels continuous. Preserve screen direction of the outgoing pulse and return; avoid spatial disorientation.

### Shot A acceptance

The viewer can locate the emitter and impacted wall, recognize outgoing and returning motion, and feel the darkness without losing visibility of the actual tool.

## Section-wide direction

## Approved engineering facts and boundaries (for all three shots)

1. **Sensor arrangement:** ENP3 has **3 outward-facing ultrasonic pulse–echo imaging sensors, 120° apart in a single axial plane**. The sensor plane is specified as 60 cm above the lower pin shoulder. The mud sensor is separate. Do not confuse round ports or the hydraulic pads with imaging sensors.
2. **Rotary operation:** In the depicted rotating drilling mode, the **whole connected drill string and ECCO body rotate together**. The client specifies clockwise rotation when viewed from surface looking toward well bottom. There is no independently spinning optical/acoustic head inside a stationary body.
3. **Echo physics:** A pulse passes through the drilling fluid to the wall and the reflected signal returns to the sensor. **Same sensor = transmit and receive**. A one-way beam does not communicate pulse–echo measurement.
4. **Distance:** Two-way travel time and corrected mud sound speed estimate the sensor-to-wall stand-off. In idealized terms `distance ≈ velocity × (echo_time − transmit_time) / 2`. Borehole radius also needs the sensor's radial position/eccentricity; a waveform alone does not directly supply absolute radius.
5. **Repeated samples:** More readings around changing azimuths yield a *set of radial wall measurements*. A full rotation contributes coverage, but one revolution is not literally a single instantaneous data 'photo'. Sampling can be dense; the color-coded 3D surface and through-depth reconstruction belong to Section 3.
6. **Open-hole environment:** Bare, irregular geological rock wall; NOT the steel casing, cement or an empty optically clear tunnel. The annular volume contains drilling mud even if visually simplified for legibility.
7. **Camera:** A virtual CGI viewpoint can show through selectively cut-away rock or mud for explanation. This transparency is *not* a literal property of the underground scene.
8. **Color and lighting:** Cyan glow is a stylized way to visualize invisible acoustic energy; the pulse does not create physical blue light underground. No sweeping blue lasers, front-mounted optical spotlights or rotating scan heads.
9. **Product accuracy:** The generated images are **cinematic inspiration**, NOT approved ENP3 CAD or authoritative sensor placement. Production must use the verified client Blender/CAD asset and confirm which actual openings correspond to imaging sensors.
10. **No invented field data:** The shot is a representation of the measurement principle, not actual Mi-VU numerical measurements or a validated performance simulation. Client engineering confirmation remains separate from aesthetic approval.

- **`S02_A_First_Contact.png`** — use for darkness, diagonally receding industrial body, close rock, isolated spot and restrained cyan effect. **Do not** copy its end-facing emit position as actual ENP3 sensor placement.
