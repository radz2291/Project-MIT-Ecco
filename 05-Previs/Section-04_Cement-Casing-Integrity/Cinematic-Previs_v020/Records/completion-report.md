# S04 v020 — recompose the complete continuous journey

## Owner request and response

The owner rejects the v019 camera angles and replaces its temporary B/C comparison window with full-screen B followed by full-screen C. The scene remains one continuous camera, 18 seconds, with a fixed 50% cutaway from frame 1. The owner explicitly requests faster animation and application of the complete AI Director component set. This authorizes the bounded revision and inexpensive previs, not final finishing.

The new premise is a guided inspection: establish the tool entering the casing, show a supported location, travel to a distinct reduced-backing location, then stay behind to inspect wear after the tool passes. Each arrival is composed as an image before connecting the path. The camera's travel changes what becomes visible rather than serving as decorative orbiting.

## Why the previous framing failed

The previous B/C camera used a portrait-oriented view inside a landscape frame, leaving much of the image dominated by the tool body. D became a narrow strip of wall or a weakly contextualized surface. A started too far away. Smooth interpolation did not solve the weak arrivals. This revision changes position, distance, orientation, lens, tool passage and information timing together; it is not just a faster version of the rejected path.

## Physical and information constraints

The v019 physical well mesh coordinates and topology remain unchanged. The permanent half-section stays fixed. C effects are imported from the approved C source and remain at its fixed world location; no second well is inserted and cement never changes at B. The world placement controller moves the real linked hardware between the two source sensor stations. After C it moves the bit beyond the wear-profile stations so the curved steel is visible, rather than hiding the tool to fabricate the view.

The authored wear remains approximately 3.998 mm maximum. Nominal references and actual worn profiles retain their source coordinates, fine dash styling and local worn-angle limits. The cool depth field is the existing computed radial difference, not a painted patch, temperature, acoustic amplitude or calibrated thickness/volume measurement. Section-face material is plain steel; the data layer is confined to the existing barrel material treatment. No bump, displacement, new deformation or new measurement numbers are introduced.

The cyan exterior region is a fixed genuine channel volume within the cement annulus, outside the steel. Its material opacity is simplified for this inexpensive proxy. It is not a surface ribbon, flowing stream or direct image obtained through steel. Source dimensions, canonical assets and previous versions are preserved.

## Verification and review limits

Representative opening, B, travel, C and D frames are rendered at working settings before batch rendering. All render-to-file times are recorded and must remain below five seconds. The owner target remains approximately one second per rendered frame; playback is 24 fps. Exact final/attempted render statistics are in delivery.json and the timing records.

Saved-scene checks compare physical mesh fingerprints with v019, verify source-file hashes and canonical casing hash, check static well transforms/visibility throughout all 432 frames, confirm one camera, and compare B/C sensor/contact projections. The native package is saved/reopened and its relative dependencies verified. The complete H.264 movie is decoded through all 432 frames. The conservative casing-annulus check is not a complete assembly collision certification.

Visual self-review includes full-size arrivals and chronological travel/reveal frames. The intended silent takeaway is: the same sensor meets steel under two different backing conditions and the responses differ; the final inner-wall geometry departs locally from its reference. This is a new composition requiring owner normal-speed review. No independent cold-viewer test, engineering certification or client acceptance is claimed.
