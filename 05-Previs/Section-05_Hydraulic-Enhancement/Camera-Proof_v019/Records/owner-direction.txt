The camera travels along the body, but the compositions do not create sufficiently distinctive moments.

We are seeing different portions of the same product without experiencing three strongly authored discoveries.

I don't want us to solve this simply by making everything wider, adding labels or increasing the number of effects.

**This is primarily a camera-direction and compositional problem.**

## 3. Proposed camera direction

Please reinterpret the approved three-part sequence with stronger visual authorship.

### A — Sculptural Arrival (0–5 seconds)

Establish ENP3 and the annular environment through a compelling spatial composition.

Rather than defaulting to a conventional wide engineering shot, explore:

- Strong foreground, middle-ground and background relationships.
- A memorable perspective on the accelerator geometry.
- Purposeful camera movement along the upward fluid direction.
- Meaningful parallax between product, fluid and borehole.
- A deliberate arrival where the accelerator becomes the visual subject.

The opening should make the engineering feel significant while keeping the fluid relationship understandable.

### B — Signature Twisted-Pad Showcase (5–12 seconds)

This should be the strongest cinematic moment of Section 5.

Use the approved hydraulic interaction from v017/v018 as the foundation.

I want a camera movement that discovers the sculpted pad geometry and its interaction with flowing fluid.

Consider a controlled approach or partial arc that changes our understanding of the geometry through silhouette and parallax.

Then settle into a powerful composition where the rotating pads, redirected fluid and transported cuttings become readable together.

The camera should feel aware of the product's form.

**The journey toward the pad should be as deliberately directed as the arrival itself.**

Avoid a generic orbit or continuous movement with no meaningful visual destination.

### C — Integrated System Revelation (12–18 seconds)

The final composition should be a cinematic payoff.

Follow the fluid toward the diffuser, then use a meaningful camera transition to reveal the connected hydraulic body.

I don't want the ending to be merely a wider view.

It should feel like a discovery:

**What we have been observing in detail is part of one integrated engineering design.**

Use spatial composition, depth, product silhouette and controlled fluid movement to give the complete system presence.

Let the camera settle into a memorable final image that can naturally hand off to Section 6.

## 4. Camera choreography requirements

Please design the key compositions **before designing the camera paths**.

For each main arrival, determine:

- What the audience sees.
- What becomes newly understandable.
- Why this viewpoint is worth showcasing.
- Where the product sits in the composition.
- How foreground and background create depth.
- How the camera approaches and leaves.
- Where movement accelerates, decelerates or settles.
- How the visual attention transfers toward the next discovery.

Do not apply identical camera motion to all three regions.

The camera journey should have rhythm and contrast.

A deliberate cut is acceptable if it produces a stronger result than continuous camera travel.
