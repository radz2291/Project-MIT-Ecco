# SECTION 04 — CASED HOLE: CEMENT EVALUATION & CASING INTEGRITY

**0:40–0:58 | Four cinematic shot references | Step 1: Shot Architecture**

## Section introduction (paste at top of the Padlet column)

After demonstrating ECCO's open-hole imaging, the film returns to a previously completed, steel-cased part of the well. The drilling assembly travels upward into existing casing. ECCO now investigates two different problems: acoustic responses that help evaluate the material supporting casing from behind, and changes in the steel's own internal shape that may indicate wear. We gradually make the normally hidden cement annulus translucent, reveal an axial fluid-filled channel on one side, then move inside casing to inspect a localized internal wear groove.

**Main message:** ECCO can support both *cement evaluation behind casing* and *casing internal geometry assessment* without pretending that ultrasound produces an optical photograph through steel.

---

### S04-A — Entering the Cased World (0:40–0:44)

**Camera composition:** A diagonal three-quarter view down a deviated well. The rough open-hole section occupies one side of the frame, while an existing smooth casing and its lower shoe are visible above the tool.

**What happens:** Following a deeper drilling stage, the BHA travels upward. ECCO moves into the previously installed casing while the drill bit remains below, establishing the transition into a different operating environment. The camera follows upward and slightly pulls back to make the casing shoe readable.

**What the audience should understand:** We have moved from exposed rock into an engineered steel-cased section. The casing is already there—it is not forming around the tool.

**End/transition:** ECCO's sensing region is within casing; move into a closer sensor-versus-steel composition.

---

### S04-B — What Lies Behind Steel (0:44–0:49)

**Camera composition:** An intimate oblique side view inside deviated steel casing, with ECCO's imaging sensor and a selectively translucent window into the annulus outside casing.

**What happens:** ECCO emits an ultrasonic pulse; the steel casing responds with ringing. Where cement supports the casing, the acoustic response decays relatively quickly; where a fluid-like or poorly bonded region sits behind it, ringing persists longer. A brief comparison graphic supports the contrast as the backing material gradually becomes visible as an explanatory overlay.

**What the audience should understand:** Differences in the returned ultrasonic response can reveal information about support behind opaque steel.

**End/transition:** One anomalous backing location remains subtly highlighted. Camera begins moving along the casing to investigate its extent.

---

### S04-C — The Hidden Channel (0:49–0:54)

**Camera composition:** A longer three-quarter side perspective down the casing. ECCO remains inside the steel, while the surrounding cement sheath is revealed using controlled partial transparency.

**What happens:** The earlier anomaly becomes an axial blue channel positioned **outside the casing** in the cement annulus, occupying one circumferential side. Warm yellow/brown cement surrounds it. The camera tracks the channel along depth without turning it into a freely flowing river or fully unwrapping it into a technical log.

**What the audience should understand:** A cement-related problem may occupy one side of the casing over an extended length, not all around it.

**End/transition:** External channel overlay fades; camera returns to the casing interior to examine the steel wall itself.

---

### S04-D — The Damage Within (0:54–0:58)

**Camera composition:** A tight grazing angle inside steel casing. The ECCO sensing region anchors one side of the frame and the slightly worn inner wall is visible further ahead.

**What happens:** As ECCO scans, a localized internal wear groove is detected. A nominal inner-wall reference remains visible while the measured inner surface recedes into the steel, representing an increased local internal radius. The outer casing shape is unchanged in this illustrative case. The camera glides along the wear zone and settles briefly.

**What the audience should understand:** ECCO can measure internal casing irregularity, including metal loss from wear caused by BHA contact in deviated wells.

**End/transition:** The scene begins withdrawing from the casing toward the ENP3 exterior, preparing Section 5's hydraulic-body reveal.
