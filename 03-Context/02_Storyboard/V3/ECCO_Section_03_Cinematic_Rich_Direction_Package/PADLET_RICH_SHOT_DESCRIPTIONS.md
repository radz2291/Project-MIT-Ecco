# Padlet Shot Descriptions — Section 03

## Section 03 — Open Hole: Geometry & Formation Imaging

This section is where ECCO's measurements begin turning into meaningful open-hole engineering information.
The viewer first learns that the real borehole does not necessarily match the intended bit size. Then the audience sees that the hole can be reconstructed through depth as a continuous 3D measured wall. Finally, the same ultrasonic echoes reveal another kind of information beyond geometry: acoustic amplitude / formation imaging.

**Key message:** ECCO reveals the true shape of the borehole and can also extract a second acoustic layer of information from the same echo measurements.

---

## Shot S03-A — Reality Revealed
**Time:** 0:22–0:28

We remain close to ECCO as the first complete circumferential reconstruction becomes meaningful. A nominal drill-bit reference is compared with the actual measured wall. Blue enlargement and a local red tight spot reveal that the real borehole does not perfectly match the intended size.

This shot teaches the first important value of ECCO: the true borehole can be larger or tighter than expected.

---

## Shot S03-B — Geometry Through Depth
**Time:** 0:28–0:34

The camera travels along the borehole so the audience sees that ECCO is reconstructing geometry through a longer interval, not just one circular slice. The wall becomes a continuous 3D measured surface with varying diameter and irregularity through depth.

This shot teaches the continuity of the measurement: ECCO reconstructs the borehole shape in three dimensions over distance.

---

## Shot S03-C — Beyond Geometry
**Time:** 0:34–0:40

The visual emphasis transitions from blue geometry interpretation into a yellow/brown amplitude or formation-imaging representation on the same measured wall surface. The viewer understands that geometry is not the only output—the same echoes provide another layer of engineering information.

This shot teaches that ECCO is not only measuring shape; it is also revealing acoustic variation in the surrounding formation.
