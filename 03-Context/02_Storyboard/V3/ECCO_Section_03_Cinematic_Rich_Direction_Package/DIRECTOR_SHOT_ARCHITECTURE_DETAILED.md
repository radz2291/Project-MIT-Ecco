# Director Shot Architecture — Section 03

**Section title:** Open Hole — Geometry & Formation Imaging  
**Timeline:** 0:22–0:40  
**Duration:** 18 seconds  
**Narrative role:** Convert the first circumferential reconstruction into meaningful engineering insight.

---

## Section-level dramatic objective

Section 2 taught the principle: ECCO sends sound, listens for the echo, and reconstructs the hidden borehole wall.

Section 3 must now reveal the first real engineering value of that measurement. The audience should understand that ECCO is not merely drawing a ring around itself. It is showing whether the drilled hole is enlarged, undersized, irregular through depth, and acoustically varied along the surrounding rock wall.

The dramatic progression is:

**What we expected → what the hole actually is → how that hole evolves through depth → how the same echo gives another layer of information.**

We want the section to feel like one continuous journey inside the measured borehole, not a disconnected series of engineering diagrams.

---

## Shot S03-A — Reality Revealed

**Time:** 0:22–0:28  
**Duration:** 6 seconds

### Cinematic purpose

Reveal the difference between the nominal drill-bit diameter and the actual measured hole.
This is the first time the viewer sees that the borehole can be enlarged or locally tight.

### Camera composition

Stay close to the end composition of Section 2. Use an oblique three-quarter perspective with ECCO visible inside the borehole. The tool should remain visually central enough that continuity from Section 2 is obvious, but the surrounding wall must now become the focus.

A white nominal reference contour or ring should appear in relation to the measured wall. The measured wall can then diverge from that nominal reference with blue enlargement and a smaller red tight-spot region.

### What happens

The circumferential reconstruction from Section 2 remains visible.
A clean nominal drill-bit reference appears.
The viewer sees that the actual measured wall does not perfectly match the nominal reference.
Blue areas indicate enlargement beyond bit size, and a small red area indicates a local tight spot.

### Camera movement

A controlled, modest continuation move. Avoid a sudden reframing. A subtle forward drift or slight rotational settle is appropriate, but the shot is mostly about **revelation through overlay** rather than motion.

### Audience experience

“Ah—this isn't just a perfect cylinder. The real hole can be larger or tighter than expected.”

### End composition

The viewer clearly understands the comparison between **expected hole size** and **actual measured geometry**.
This end frame should be strong enough to hand off naturally into a longer-through-depth reveal.

### Key caution

The current generated image is useful for tone, depth, and atmosphere, but it still needs the nominal white reference to be explicit if used as a direct shot target.

---

## Shot S03-B — Geometry Through Depth

**Time:** 0:28–0:34  
**Duration:** 6 seconds

### Cinematic purpose

Show that ECCO reconstructs geometry through depth, not just at one isolated cross-section.
The audience should feel the borehole becoming a larger 3D measured structure.

### Camera composition

Pull back and travel along the borehole axis so a longer stretch of reconstructed wall becomes visible. The frame should still be cinematic and spatial, not flattened into a data board.

The borehole wall itself becomes the subject. The shot should clearly suggest that surface variations belong to the borehole wall, not to an artificial spiral object floating around the tool.

### What happens

Successive circumferential measurements connect into a continuous 3D wall reconstruction.
The borehole shows varying diameter along depth, localized enlargement, and possibly subtle spiral-shaped irregularity if we want to suggest drilling-induced distortion.

The key message is continuity through depth.

### Camera movement

A steady pullback with a slight axial glide. The feeling should be: the measurement is growing into a larger revealed space.

### Audience experience

“ECCO can reconstruct the actual borehole surface through a longer interval, not just one circular slice.”

### End composition

The audience now sees a recognizable 3D borehole surface with variations through depth.
This creates the perfect setup to introduce a second property of the same echoes.

### Key caution

The spiral irregularity, if present, must be perceived as a pattern in the wall geometry itself. Do **not** represent it as a thick blue ribbon floating independently around the tool.

---

## Shot S03-C — Beyond Geometry

**Time:** 0:34–0:40  
**Duration:** 6 seconds

### Cinematic purpose

Introduce the idea that geometry is not the only thing ECCO obtains. The same ultrasonic return also provides an acoustic property that can be rendered as formation/amplitude imaging.

### Camera composition

Remain in the same overall 3D environment. Do not immediately cut to a flat software report. Instead, stay emotionally and spatially continuous with Shot B.

The camera should move closer to the reconstructed wall or hold a strong angle on that wall while the displayed information transitions.

### What happens

The geometry emphasis recedes. A yellow/brown amplitude or formation-imaging representation emerges across the same measured wall surface.
The audience should understand that the underlying measured wall is the same, but the information being visualized has changed.

### Camera movement

Minimal. Let the visual transformation carry the meaning. A slow settle or slight push can work, but the key action is the shift from blue geometry interpretation into yellow/brown amplitude interpretation.

### Audience experience

“ECCO is not only measuring shape—it is also telling us something about the surrounding formation response.”

### End composition

The borehole wall now carries the amplitude / formation-imaging look, preparing us to leave open hole and later transition toward cased-hole applications.

### Key caution

The rock itself is not literally changing color. This is a representational transition between two different interpretations of the ultrasonic data.

## Engineering truths to preserve

These cinematic references must remain subordinate to the approved mechanical understanding:

- The nominal bit size is the reference for hole enlargement/tightness.
- White indicates nominal hole size.
- Blue indicates enlargement; darker blue indicates greater enlargement.
- Red indicates the radius is smaller than bit size (tight spot).
- Geometry through depth is a reconstruction of measured borehole wall shape.
- Spiral irregularity, if shown, is a borehole-wall phenomenon, not a decorative ribbon.
- Yellow/brown amplitude imagery is another property derived from the same echoes.
- Section 6 is still where full engineering-output presentation and unwrapped 2D reporting are explained more explicitly.

## Handoff note for downstream agents

If a 3D storyboard or Blender agent uses this package, they should treat it as **cinematic direction** rather than literal design lock.
The board determines:
- emotional and visual purpose,
- camera intent,
- continuity,
- what the audience must understand.

The board does **not** yet fully determine:
- final camera coordinates,
- exact animation curves,
- exact CAD-faithful tool geometry,
- final compositing treatment.

Those belong in the next stage.
