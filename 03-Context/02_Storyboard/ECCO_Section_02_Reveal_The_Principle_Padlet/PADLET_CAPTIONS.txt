ECCO ENP3 — SECTION 2: REVEAL THE PRINCIPLE (0:10–0:22)

Panel 1 — First pulse (0:10–0:12)
ECCO begins the measurement process by sending one ultrasonic pulse outward toward the borehole wall. This introduces the basic sensing principle.

Panel 2 — Echo returns (0:12–0:14)
The pulse reflects from the borehole wall and returns to ECCO. This is the crucial pulse-echo step: ECCO measures by transmitting and then listening.

Panel 3 — One measurement point (0:14–0:16)
One pulse and one returned echo produce one measured point on the wall. At this stage, ECCO is not creating an optical image — it is building measurement data.

Panel 4 — More pulses while rotating (0:16–0:19)
As the drill string rotates, ECCO rotates with it. More pulses are sent in different directions, and more measured points accumulate around the borehole wall.

Panel 5 — One complete ring (0:19–0:22)
After enough rotation and repeated pulse-echo measurements, one circular ring of wall data is formed at that depth. This is the basic unit of ECCO’s borehole reconstruction.

SECTION INTRODUCTION
In darkness, ECCO reveals how sensing makes the unseen environment readable. One ultrasonic pulse travels outward, reflects from the borehole wall and returns; one pulse-echo pair yields one measured point. As the drill string rotates, repeated measurements accumulate into a circular ring of wall data — the basic unit of ECCO’s borehole reconstruction.

CLIENT ENGINEERING REVIEW NOTE
The pulse, echo and rotating-scan behaviour shown here is the simplified sensing principle used for storytelling. Emission geometry, pulse repetition, rotation rate and the visual treatment of measurement points must be reconciled with MIT’s technical explanation and the engineering baseline before final animation. No claim is made about specific sensor hardware, frequencies or data rates.
