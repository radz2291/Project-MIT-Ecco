ECCO ENP3 — SECTION 6 (OPTION 2): FROM INVISIBLE MEASUREMENTS TO REAL ENGINEERING OUTPUT (1:10–1:22)

This section was revised after client feedback. Only the final panel shows the real Mi-VU report. Panels 1–4 remain cinematic and stylized.

Panel 1 — The measurements are retrieved (1:10–1:12)
After ECCO is retrieved from the well, its stored ultrasonic measurements are downloaded for processing.
We visualize the invisible recorded information as streams of light emerging from the tool.

This is a cinematic representation of stored data being accessed, not real-time transmission from underground.

Panel 2 — Measurements begin forming structure (1:12–1:15)
The stored ultrasonic measurements are processed and organized according to their recorded positions and depths.

Abstract points begin forming a continuous representation of the borehole, showing how individual measurements can contribute to an understanding of the underground structure.

This is a stylized data visualization, not a literal view of the Mi-VU software.

Panel 3 — Two different types of information (1:15–1:18)
ECCO's ultrasonic recordings support two complementary forms of analysis.
The measurements of echo travel time can be used to reconstruct borehole geometry, while the strength of the returning signal can be mapped to reveal acoustic variations along the formation.

In this scene, the two outputs appear side by side as stylized visualizations: blue for geometry and warm tones for amplitude.

They are separate interpretations of recorded echoes, not one image being converted into the other.

Panel 4 — The visualizations come together (1:18–1:20)
The stylized geometry and amplitude visualizations converge toward a single presentation of the processed measurements.

This is the transition from our cinematic explanation to MIT's real engineering software.

At the end of the shot, we move directly into actual Mi-VU field output.

Panel 5 — Reveal the actual Mi-VU field log (1:20–1:22)
The final frame reveals an actual engineering report generated by MIT's Mi-VU software using recorded ECCO-family measurements.
The report brings together borehole diameter, circular cross-sections, 3D hole geometry, 360° hole-size images, variance and amplitude in one engineering presentation.

This creates a direct connection between the sensing principles demonstrated in the animation and their practical engineering applications.

Reference: LipanBara field report, ENP1. The supplied data should be used with MIT's approval.

SECTION INTRODUCTION
The key is the contrast between Panel 4 and Panel 5. We spend the first ten seconds in a carefully designed visual world, then suddenly see the real field log. That gives the engineering evidence more impact without making the entire sequence look like a software demonstration.

ENGINEERING REVIEW NOTE
ECCO is a memory-logging tool. This sequence is about stored measurements being processed after retrieval, not a live 3D image streaming from underground.

The LipanBara field report supplied by Ahmed identifies ENP1, not ENP3. We may use it as an actual ECCO-family result, subject to MIT's approval, but we should not claim it is ENP3 field data.
