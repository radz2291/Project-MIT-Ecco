ECCO ENP3 — SECTION 3: OPEN HOLE — REVEAL THE RAW EARTH (0:22–0:40)

This section introduces ECCO's first two capabilities: borehole geometry and formation imaging.

Panel 1 — Ideal borehole diameter (0:22–0:25)
We begin with the expected borehole diameter based on the drill bit size. The circular reference represents the intended hole size before comparing it with the actual underground conditions.

Panel 2 — Actual borehole geometry / washout (0:25–0:29)
As drilling progresses, the surrounding formation may break away or become enlarged, creating an irregular hole known as washout.

ECCO measures the distance to the wall in different directions, allowing the actual borehole shape to be compared with the intended diameter. The highlighted section represents an enlarged area.

Panel 3 — Geometry through depth (0:29–0:32)
As ECCO rotates and travels through the well, measurements are collected at successive depths.

These measurements are combined to reconstruct the changing borehole geometry, including enlarged, narrowed or irregular sections.

The result is a continuous 3D representation of the actual drilled hole.

Panel 4 — The same echo provides another measurement (0:32–0:35)
The same ultrasonic echo used to calculate distance also carries information about the strength of the reflected signal.
ECCO analyses two properties:
• Time of Flight: How long the echo takes to return, used to determine distance and borehole geometry.
• Amplitude: The strength of the reflected echo, providing information about acoustic variations along the formation wall.

These are two different measurements extracted from the ultrasonic response.

Panel 5 — Amplitude becomes a formation image (0:35–0:40)
As ECCO rotates and moves through depth, amplitude measurements accumulate around the borehole wall.

These measurements are arranged into a continuous 360° image, displayed using different color shades to represent variations in the ultrasonic reflections.

The resulting formation image helps geologists identify patterns and changes in the surrounding rock. The colors represent measured acoustic responses, not the actual color of the formation.

IMPORTANT POINT FOR AHMED'S REVIEW
Panels 3 and 5 show two separate outputs from the same sensing system. We are not suggesting that geometry is converted into amplitude. The transition is about revealing another property of the returned signal.

These panels are conceptual diagrams for confirming the story mechanics, not exact representations of ECCO's final hardware or processed field data.
