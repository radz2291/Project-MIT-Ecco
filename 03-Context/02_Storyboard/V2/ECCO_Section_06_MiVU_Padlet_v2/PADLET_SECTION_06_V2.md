## Section introduction — Padlet-ready

This sequence connects ECCO's ultrasonic recordings to engineering outputs. We leave the downhole tool and follow its stored measurements into a reconstructed 3D borehole surface. That curved surface is opened conceptually along its axis, then flattened into a 2D printable representation: circumferential position (azimuth) across the page, and measured depth along the page. Different measurements can be placed on this same 2D coordinate system, including hole-size geometry and acoustic amplitude. Only the final panel reveals a real Mi-VU field-log example.

**Core message:** The 2D log can be understood as an unwrapped view of 360° measurements represented on the 3D wellbore surface.

We leave the physical downhole tool as its recorded measurement points are interpreted to reveal the shape of the well. ## Panel 2 — Complete reconstructed 3D borehole (1:12–1:14)
**SVG:** `02_3D_Reconstructed_Borehole.svg`

Measurements around the borehole at successive depths combine into a continuous representation of the irregular drilled hole. White indicates the nominal drill-bit size; increasing blue intensity represents borehole enlargement; red highlights locally undersized/tight regions. The geometry may show spiral deviations linked to drilling behaviour as well as washout. Color thresholds and exact rendering style are for MIT to approve. The 'rings' are cross-sectional position samples in a continuous surface, not independent physical hoops.

## Panel 3 — Split the 3D surface along a longitudinal seam (1:14–1:17)
**SVG:** `03_Vertical_Seam_Opening.svg`

To explain the relationship between 3D and 2D, we draw a vertical seam along the RECONSTRUCTED borehole surface and open the representation, like unrolling a cylindrical sleeve. The seam is a graphic visualization; nothing is physically cut underground. The opening is along the tool / borehole axis, not around a horizontal circumference.

## Panel 4 — Flatten into a 2D printable measurement map (1:17–1:20)
**SVG:** `04_Unwrapped_2D_Measurement_Maps.svg`

The opened 3D surface becomes a flat map. Horizontal direction represents 360 degrees around the borehole; vertical direction represents progress through measured depth. The larger blue/white/red panel illustrates a hole-size geometry map. A separate yellow/brown panel illustrates the amplitude measurement over the same azimuth and depth coordinates. The two tracks are complementary recorded properties, *not* one image transforming into another. This is a stylized map, not actual Mi-VU data.
