## Section introduction — Padlet-ready

Section 4 changes the operating environment from the open rock wall into the previously installed, cemented steel casing. After the BHA has drilled the deeper open-hole section, the drill bit and ECCO travel upward into the existing casing. ECCO uses ultrasonic returns to examine two different aspects of well integrity: how the casing rings when it is supported or poorly supported by material outside it, and how its INTERNAL geometry may have changed through wear. Ahmed's requested side/deviated-well perspective is used to clarify these mechanics. The cement map is shown with axial channels on one side of the casing: yellow/brown for cement and blue for water outside casing, indicating absent cement in this conceptual example.

**Core message:** The same family of ultrasonic measurements helps investigate cement support BEHIND the casing and the condition of the casing's INNER surface.

## Panel 2 — Ultrasonic interaction with casing (0:43–0:46)
**SVG:** `02_Ultrasonic_Response_in_Casing.svg`

In a deviated cased well, ECCO sends an ultrasonic pulse through the well fluid to the INNER steel casing wall. The returned response includes the reflected signal and characteristics of how the casing wall vibrates or rings. The pulse and casing response are artistically visualized so viewers can understand where the acoustic information originates; the tool is not sending light through the steel.

## Panel 3 — Cement changes casing ringing: deviated side view (0:46–0:50)
**SVG:** `03_Deviated_Well_Cement_Ringing.svg`

We compare two lengthwise side views in a deviated well instead of top-down cross-sections. On one side, cement adequately supports the steel casing; this generally damps the ultrasonic casing response faster. On the other side, a water-filled or poorly supported annular channel behind the casing allows a more persistent ringing response. The waveforms are illustrative; the exact interpretation depends on operating conditions and signal processing. These drawings should not imply a single waveform alone proves cement quality.

## Panel 4 — Axial cement and water channels in the 360° map (0:50–0:54)
**SVG:** `04_Axial_Cement_Water_Channel.svg`

ECCO's measurements around the casing and through depth become a circumferential cement evaluation map. Following Ahmed's direction, a blue WATER channel runs lengthwise along ONE SIDE outside the casing, while the surrounding yellow/brown indicates cement. The channel extends along the casing's AXIS (through depth), not as a horizontal ring wrapping around the entire casing. The colors represent the requested cement/water interpretation, not the geometry or formation-amplitude scales used elsewhere in the storyboard. Final data colors and exact channel extent require MIT confirmation.

## Panel 5 — Inner-wall groove enlarges casing internal diameter (0:54–0:58)
**SVG:** `05_Inner_Casing_Wear_Groove.svg`

We compare the normal casing INNER surface with a localized wear groove made by the drill string rubbing the inner side during drilling of a deeper interval, particularly in a deviated well. The groove starts at the INNER wall and cuts INTO the steel; the casing's OUTER diameter remains the same in this example. At the worn section, the measured distance from ECCO to the inner wall increases locally, indicating a larger internal radius and local metal loss. This is an internal geometry measurement, not a direct measurement of the casing exterior.
