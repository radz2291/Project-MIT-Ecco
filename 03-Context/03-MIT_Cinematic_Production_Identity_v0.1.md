## 3. Visual identity pillars

### A. Product as the hero
- Preserve approved geometry, dimensions, feature placement, cross-sectional relationships and mechanical movements.
- Use identifiable product details rather than inventing decorative machinery.
- Hero framing may be dramatic, but never obscure a feature that the shot is meant to explain.
- For ECCO ENP3, the approved CAD geometry is the visual authority, subject to technical client verification. Earlier presentation plugs/cores are temporary visual closures, **not** validated internals.

### B. Precision + tactility
- Premium, believable machined surfaces: fine finish, controlled reflections, plausible edge definition and subtle manufacturing texture.
- Avoid dirty, damaged, overscratched or plastic-looking finishes.
- Do **not** assert a specific physical material just because the image generator made it resemble titanium or stainless steel. The ENP3 documentation specifies a non-magnetic body; the actual material/finish requires client confirmation.

### C. Deliberate cinematography
- Prefer purposeful 3D camera movement, spatial parallax, expressive angles, careful lens perspective and strong silhouette.
- Dramatic slanted compositions are an option, not a mandatory template.
- Camera moves should reveal information, not merely orbit for decoration.
- A single continuous shot should preserve camera-path continuity; treat fast-cut or multi-camera sequences shot by shot.

### D. Revelation, not decoration
- Technical effects should represent a *specific observable or inferred process*: where a pulse begins, how it propagates, what it contacts, and what information appears.
- A wall reveal should be caused by the arriving wavefront; if a measured environment appears, it should remain visible as the shot resolves.
- Avoid generic neon rings, atomic/gyroscope shapes, unnecessary HUDs, particles and floating patches.
- Distinguish metaphorical visualization from engineering-validated physics.

### E. Restrained visual sophistication
- Balance clean negative space, soft atmosphere and cinematic contrast.
- Avoid overload, heavy sci-fi gloss, neon-blue clichés and indiscriminate text overlays.
- Technical graphics should be minimal, legible and traceable to approved meaning.

## 4. Style territories: flexible environments, consistent discipline

These are **working artistic territories**, not official MIT brand-color definitions.

| Territory | Role | Typical treatment | Important restriction |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Depth** | Concealment, subsurface location, unknown conditions | Deep mineral charcoal/green, low-key light, sparse spatial cues | Do not make the product or technical phenomenon unreadable |
| **Precision** | Product presentation and detailed mechanisms | Neutral/off-white or subdued sage environments, tactile metallic highlights | Do not falsely depict physical metallurgy |
| **Revelation** | Sensing, measurement, mapping and insight | Restrained sage/green-white accents, translucent geometry, meaningful contours | Do not use light solely as decoration |

Transition between territories as storytelling requires. Do not force green across every shot.

## 5. Consistent visual grammar

### Product photography
- Beauty: authored camera and light, clean machined texture, high-quality separation from the background.
- Technical: framing that exposes the correct mechanism and its relation to the borehole/casing.
- Hero: allow negative space and a final stable composition for the product name or brand signoff.

### Sensing and measurement visualization
- Identify an origin, a traveling/active effect, an interaction and an outcome.
- Favor thin, spatially readable effects; use an expanding surface only where the story requires it.
- Preserve occlusion and scale between tool, pulse and wall.
- End on the intended information state, not an arbitrary flourish.

### Environment
- Foreground product, local interaction volume and more distant atmospheric setting should be distinguishable.
- Background geometry can support parallax, but must not be confused with the actual borehole.
- Geological/casing structures must be derived from approved technical scenarios when presented as factual.

### Typography and diagrams
- Establish a small hierarchy for title, feature name, annotated callout and measurement.
- Prefer sparse, editorial typography and accurate labels over default floating-HUD styling.
- Any numerical readout, waveform, sensor placement or geological conclusion needs an identified and approved source.

### Sound
- Optional signature: quiet acoustic impulse / mechanical resonance / controlled decay; silence and dynamic range matter.
- Sound should support the event shown (e.g. pulse and reflection) without implying inaccurate sensor behavior.
- Avoid bombastic trailers or generic perpetual corporate build-ups.

## 6. Hybrid execution standard — discoveries from the ECCO tests

### Source-of-truth hierarchy
1. **Client-approved technical reference**: authoritative for mechanism, claims, physical constraints.
2. **Blender scene and reference animation**: authoritative for controllable geometry, spatial movement and shot timing.
3. **AI-generated appearance anchors**: authoritative for the intended creative finish, not product redesign.
4. **Generated video + composite**: proposed output, subject to fidelity checks and corrections.

### What Blender must encode
- The correct product asset and its visible engineering details.
- Camera path, parallax, framing and easing.
- Essential effect origin, direction, contact, state change and timing.
- Readable spatial layers and object occlusion.
- Editable modular separation: product, borehole, effects, camera and background.

Do **not** demand complete photorealistic Blender rendering for every AI-finished shot. However, use enough shading and distinguishable geometry to avoid ambiguous motion guidance.

### Appearance-anchor rule — important proven working observation
For **single-camera continuous shots**, our experiments found that AI-generated **first frame, last frame, or both** were generally better reference choices than one isolated generated middle frame:
- **First**: lock the appearance of the opening; allow the model to interpolate later states.
- **Last**: lock the visual destination, especially valuable when the effect/reveal is the point of the scene.
- **First + last**: control both intended boundary states where the model allows it and where the two images are mutually consistent.
- **Middle anchors**: use only when image-to-image continuity is reliable enough not to introduce contradictory geometry, lighting, materials or scene identity.

This is an **observed workflow heuristic**, not a universal model guarantee. For fast-cut or multi-camera films, treat every shot separately; a middle *timeline* frame may actually be the first or last anchor for another shot.

### Prompt method
- Prompts explain the artistic result in calm, natural language and distinguish motion authority from finishing authority.
- Preserve product silhouette, camera and spatial state without excessive redundant negative lists.
- Use short, meaningful appearance direction: refined material, light, atmosphere, clean depth.
- When the model drifts, first diagnose whether the source animation is visually ambiguous or the generative edit is unconstrained. Don't rewrite longer prompts endlessly.

### What to preserve for corrective compositing
Where practical, export source renders or masks/passes for product geometry, wavefronts, reveal state, depth and motion. Reintroduce critical exact details during compositing if video generation distorts them.

## 7. Lessons from the 4-second ECCO prototype

| Observed issue | Working lesson |
|---|---|
| Three orthogonal rings read like an atomic symbol | Encode meaningful propagation, not generic abstract forms |
| Green wireframe/cage and rectangular patches | Simplify the borehole to clear spatial volumes and restrained contours |
| Initial ending lost the revealed wall | Preserve the discovery in the last frame |
| Added curved distant background | Parallax cues help depth, but distinguish background from local borehole |
| Image-to-image finishing improved the still | Develop visual appearance from a real Blender frame |
| Generated middle image caused uncertain beginning/ending | Prefer boundary-frame appearance anchors for continuous shots unless middle frames are truly consistent |

## 8. Technical fidelity: ECCO-specific guardrails

For the initial film, storyboard and imaging assertions should be checked against the engineering datasheet and Mr. Ahmed's confirmed explanations:
- ECCO ENP3 is a memory-while-drilling ultrasonic pulse-echo imaging system with integrated hydraulic enhancement.
- The documentation describes **three imaging sensors, 120° apart** on a sensor plane, and a dedicated mud sensor.
- Important application narratives include open-hole geometry/formation indications, cement evaluation, casing condition and hydraulic hole cleaning.
- The proprietary hydraulic module contains accelerator, twisted-pad and diffuser stages; their representations need engineering approval.
- Current toy wavefront shapes in the sample are **artistic guides**, not validated acoustic field simulation.
- Product closures added purely for visualization should not be misrepresented as actual engineered components.
- Mi-VU sample data previously discussed relates to an ENP1 test and needs authorization/context before any ENP3 attribution.

Client should review mechanism, sequence, sensor placement, acoustic action, data-to-insight transformations and tool motion before final costly finishing.

## 12. Minimum practical brand review checklist

Before approving a shot's design:
- [ ] Is ECCO hardware recognizable and mechanically faithful?
- [ ] Is the depicted engineering process or measurement understandable?
- [ ] Are technical claims and simulated effects properly distinguished?
- [ ] Does the camera move reveal something useful?
- [ ] Can tool, local effect and distant environment be clearly separated?
- [ ] Does the shot use coherent materials, color, typography and technical graphics?
- [ ] Does the final state communicate the intended outcome?
- [ ] Are AI appearance anchors selected deliberately (first/last/both for continuous shots)?
- [ ] Is there a practical recovery path if AI changes critical geometry or timing?
- [ ] Have relevant client feedback and engineering questions been resolved or flagged?
