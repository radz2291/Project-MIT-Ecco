# Scene Casebook — Lessons from the ECCO Collaboration

These are process lessons drawn from the owner's supplied development conversation through Sections 1–3, not an engineering audit or a new approval of any scene version. The companion skill and project documents explain the applicable methods. No confidential field images, source measurements or product assets are included here.

## 1. Beautiful mechanics are not yet a film

The initial illustrations made the physical story easier to discuss. They did not establish the final lens, framing, atmosphere or motion. Generated cinematic images then helped with appearance but introduced invented product forms and ambiguous sensing locations.

**Rule:** keep mechanism explanation, art reference and executable scene direction separate. A diagram need not be a camera storyboard; an attractive image is not verified CAD. Inspect the actual scene before assigning final transforms.

## 2. A continuous experience does not require one production scene

A rapid continuous descent was useful for scale and discovery. Later, forcing the same camera/world into a scientific comparison restricted the available composition. An independent section connected by a lateral motion match offered better control.

**Rule:** decide continuity by the viewer's experience and evidence identity. Choose shared scene, separate shot or editorial match deliberately. Do not force a single camera path as a universal cinematic rule.

## 3. The result can matter more than every repeated operation

The scanning sequence initially tried to visibly acquire enough samples to earn a complete circumference. Revealing an existing history while a small local scan continued made the accumulated result easier to show.

**Rule:** preserve the essential causal chain, then compress repetition honestly. A prebuilt history is a presentation shortcut, not proof of instantaneous full-volume acquisition.

## 4. Current and historical information need one visual language

Fresh patches and historical markers initially looked unrelated. Unifying their base element and varying age, brightness and connectivity made accumulation easier to understand.

**Rule:** define the lifecycle before implementing effects: temporary encounter, received response, committed result, settled history. Keep committed information attached to its source location.

## 5. A desired category may be absent from the example

The source wall demonstrated enlargement but lacked a clearly distinct tight region under its preview tolerance. Asking repeatedly for a red mark could not create a justified classification. A separately approved illustrative variant made the example possible while preserving its limitations.

**Rule:** inspect values before judging appearance. Do not paint a diagnosis onto unrelated geometry. Separate source evidence from an educational example, check physical implications, and magnify a small difference rather than silently increasing it.

## 6. Repeated texture revisions were aimed at the wrong cause

The amplitude image looked folded. Initial comments suspected material shading or geometry. Controlled tests later showed that tonal structure in the image and a near-end-on projection could produce the effect even on an ideal cylinder and an unlit material.

**Rule:** distinguish observed appearance from verified cause. Retire a disproved diagnosis. Test the same image across controlled surfaces/views before changing another shader or mesh.

## 7. References should arrive before repeated styling guesses

A genuine-looking source crop clarified the desired visual vocabulary more effectively than repeated requests for "more scientific" texture. The later request for visibly larger screen-like cells was a new stylistic preference, not evidence that all earlier textures were scientifically false.

**Rule:** approve a concrete reference and comparable test scale early. State what the reference proves and what it cannot prove. Display pixel size is not instrument resolution. Generated detail remains illustrative.

## 8. Local feedback and acceptance must survive the next session

Direct work with Codex changed the Section 2 ending while the earlier discussion still described a point-only result. Later handoffs needed the actual scene, not an old summary. Several candidates were rejected while useful components remained valid.

**Rule:** resume from the latest accepted decisions and exact working artifacts, not a version number alone. Record changes at their real scope. Keep reviews short, plain and specific so the human can decide quickly.

## What not to transfer

No particular borehole shape, radius, palette, sensor count, shader graph, pixel style, camera orbit, fixed duration or reference studio is a universal outcome of these lessons. Future projects inherit the decision method, not ECCO's appearance or technical claims.
