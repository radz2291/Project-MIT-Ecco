# Example — Continuous Camera Discovery

**Origin:** transferable lesson from the supplied ECCO Section 1 collaboration. Not a universal camera recipe or a new production approval.

## The scene in ordinary language

We start where a person could stand, travel into a place no physical camera could reach, and stop where the next discovery can begin.

## Establish it

**01 — Meaning:** establish scale and curiosity. Decide what must be understood at the destination; do not inherit the destination from a reference clip.

**02 — Truth:** identify real connected structures, permitted virtual access and the actual product. A portfolio reference may end inside a fictional valve; the real project may need to end outside a sensor. Copying that mechanism would be wrong even if the camera is compelling.

**03 — Representation:** realistic opening, then a motivated section/occlusion/ghosting transition. Retain a recognizable spatial anchor. Decide whether a hidden cut is acceptable.

**04 — Look:** choose how reality becomes a simplified explanation. A polished reference sets finishing intent, not a requirement for expensive volume simulation during blocking.

**05 — Motion:** compose the beginning and destination first. Design the changing scale, approach, strongest acceleration, deceleration and final reading interval. Precise axial alignment may guide travel, but a side offset can preserve parallax and avoid obstructing the view.

**06 — Proof:** with permission, use cylinders/blocks and a quick playblast to test route, speed and arrival. Inspect intermediate frames as well as endpoints. Do not build a detailed rig before the journey works.

## A useful owner-facing proposal

"We will feel the descent instead of watching several separate views of it. The well gives us a clear path. We slow beside the sensor so the next pulse is easy to see. First I will test the journey with simple shapes, not finished models."

## What the test must answer

Does the viewer stay oriented? Does the change in scale feel intentional? Does the camera arrive where the next scene needs it? Is there a meaningful view during travel, not only a dramatic endpoint? Does an apparent continuous journey need one technical scene, or can an editorial match do the job better?

Keep the working move when those questions pass. Do not later force this continuous-camera solution onto a scene whose comparison would be clearer with a cut.
