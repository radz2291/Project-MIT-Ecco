## Operating procedure

1. Resolve technical authority versus open assumptions. Do not transform unresolved facts into approved scenes.
2. Define what the viewer must understand/feel and state the original core image idea.
3. Author first/key/last compositions. Critique if the important stills communicate without extra spectacle.
4. Score a purposeful camera journey through meaningful arrivals. Motion between arrivals must do something narratively useful; static views and cuts remain options.
5. Choose lighting, atmosphere, surface depiction and depth from the scene purpose and source assets. Mark taste hypotheses separately from confirmed evidence.
6. Produce cheap diagrams (SVG), camera score and initial scene contract. Use exact transforms only from known scene coordinates.
7. Give a short alternative/critique when an actual creative fork exists; retain the chosen solution's rationale.
8. Handoff to Blender/Codex with checkable conditions and low-cost playblast first. Existing `blender-animation-reference` handles packaging **after** animation is constructed.
9. Assess actual frames and playblast before claiming camera timing, production quality or approval. Capture director feedback into the evidence register if expressly authorized.

## Explicitly deferred

Typography, motion graphics, type animation and visual data graphics **do not have a chosen Rolladice aesthetic yet**. Preserve existing required technical content and client brand rules; carry style questions as TBD instead of fabricating taste. Do not let the omission cancel required titles or labels.

## Output and evidence discipline

Follow the full [scene direction contract](../../03_Directorial_Identity/07-SCENE_DIRECTION_CONTRACT_v0.2.md). Every proposal distinguishes **confirmed preference, assistant hypothesis, technical fact and unresolved question**. No claimed rendered frames, timed curves, lens choices, approval or geometry unless actually inspected. Maintain separation of taste, client technical facts and execution artifacts.
