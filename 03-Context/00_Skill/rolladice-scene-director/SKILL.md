## Steps

- Establish source-grounded technical/narrative objective and explicitly mark uncertainty.
- **Identify the actual audience and its prior knowledge before visualizing the mechanism.** Define in plain language what a non-specialist should notice, understand and find consequential, alongside the technical relationship an expert must recognize as faithful. If the audience is mixed, serve both in a readable sequence rather than forcing all engineering details into one frame.
- **Lead with the discovery, not merely the operation:** identify the baseline or question, the source-driven reveal, the meaningful difference or consequence, and the audience's short takeaway. Use a comparison only when it genuinely helps; do not assume every shot needs expected-vs-actual geometry.
- Select a strong compositional premise and meaningful key views.
- Develop camera arrivals and narratively active travel, or intentionally choose cuts/stillness.
- Choose lighting/material/depth treatment to support the actual view and object.
- For engineering data/claims, identify whether each informational beat is observed, derived, simulated, operational or illustrative; preserve data provenance, scale, precision and the client review status in the existing scene contract.
- When the scene needs text/graphics, choose concise meaningful wording and a calm sans-serif editorial starting point **if context and client identity permit**. Consider no text, screen-space overlay, or spatial type; favor legibility and composition. Any motion may be a gentle fade or graceful controlled arrival; no fixed curves. For technical graphics, choose spatial integration when it meaningfully enriches the mechanism, camera and imagery; simple on-screen graphics are equally valid. **For the S02 graphics study specifically, E016 supersedes E015: C — Spatial Cinematic is the current conceptual preference, with A1 — Pure Editorial Minimal appropriate for simpler beats.** This is not a universal visual rule; validate technical claims, spatial readability and motion in previs.
- Use SVG/previs to expose ambiguous geometry before expensive scene work.
- Critique major artistic decisions, including whether the frame speaks and the camera's travel has purpose.
- **Run a non-specialist comprehension check on the actual moving preview.** Without giving the viewer a technical explanation first, can someone state (1) what changed or was discovered and (2) why it matters? Then separately check with domain authority whether the same visuals preserve the correct physical/data meaning. If the image is only a beautiful mechanism display, redirect hierarchy, camera arrival, comparison or information hold before adding polish. Optional narration/labels may clarify but must not disguise a meaningless image.
- Deliver **one** [Scene Direction Contract](../../03_Directorial_Identity/07-SCENE_DIRECTION_CONTRACT_v0.2.md) with stage and handoff, not an additional parallel shot specification.
- Build/inspect a playblast and obtain real feedback before updating any preference as accepted.

## Boundaries

**Audience translation is not engineering simplification-by-fabrication.** Do not exaggerate source-derived geometry, manufacture unsourced performance/business benefits, or call synthetic signals real measurements. Keep source provenance and uncertainty in Module 09 and the same Scene Direction Contract.

**Typography has an initial evidenced taste profile; technical motion-graphics aesthetics are still mostly undiscovered.** Read module 08 where relevant and treat fonts, technical linework, palette and graphic animations as unapproved choices. Preserve required client labels and exact language. Do not assert camera poses or physics without inspecting actual geometry. Do not copy referenced studios' distinctive work.

For finishing an already animated Blender scene, use [blender-animation-reference](../blender-animation-reference/SKILL.md)—a separate skill and step.
