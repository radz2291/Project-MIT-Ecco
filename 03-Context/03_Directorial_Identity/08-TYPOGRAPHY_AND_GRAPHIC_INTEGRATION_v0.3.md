# Rolladice — Typography & Graphic Integration Taste

**Module:** v0.3 · 14 October 2026  
**Status:** First evidence-backed typography direction; **not** a complete motion-graphics style system  
**Owner:** Rolladice creative direction, applicable across projects; client brand and engineering authorities remain higher priority.

## Purpose and authority

This is the **single active home for typography and emerging graphic-integration taste**. Direct quotations and reference reactions live only in the [Taste Evidence Register](02-TASTE_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md) (E006–E014). This document interprets them as practical preferences; the [Scene Direction Contract](07-SCENE_DIRECTION_CONTRACT_v0.2.md) owns the handoff fields and approval gates.

Do not misread a preferred treatment as a required one. Choose graphics for the actual story, product, viewer and visual context. Existing client typography/identity specifications, technical labeling and factual constraints always take precedence.

## T01 — Quiet, modern editorial typography

**Directly preferred:** Quiet, elegant typography rather than the bold, emphatic example in a specific A/B comparison (E010); modern **sans-serif editorial** rather than the serif example in another comparison (E011).

**Working interpretation:** Start from a visually calm, contemporary and sophisticated text treatment, where meaningful wording and its placement create presence without demanding unnecessary attention.

**Not a fixed rule:** No preferred font family, weight, numeric tracking, type size, alignment, case, palette or brand system has been selected. Bold or serif typography is not forbidden when deliberately justified by another narrative/brand context.

## T02 — Wording and composition are the primary storytelling tools

**Directly preferred:** BUCK's ability to convey storytelling, elegance and sophistication through text (E008), with **composition and wording** specifically identified as the most appealing dimension (E009).

**Working interpretation:**
- Write a meaningful line first. Do not add copy simply to populate empty space.
- Compose the text relative to the subject, scene depth, negative space and visual hierarchy.
- Seek readability, meaningful breathing room and a still frame that makes sense without animation.
- A well-positioned phrase can carry an emotional beat; typography need not outperform the subject.

**Context limitation:** This preference was expressed for **storytelling-oriented films**, not as a requirement to insert narration-style statements into technical MIT mechanism shots.

## T03 — Subtle entrance is sufficient

**Directly preferred:** Both **A: quiet appearance/fade** and **B: graceful controlled movement that settles into composition** (E012).

**Working interpretation:** Choose a gentle appearance or understated movement based on pacing and the surrounding camera/image. Allow the words to settle and be read; a complex transition is not a measure of quality.

**Not established:** Exact fade length, easing curve, transform distance, per-character animation, or preference for/against editorial hard cuts. Test in context; do not invent a universal motion preset.

## T04 — Text belongs to the visual composition

**Directly preferred:** **A: typography within the imagery**, rather than the standalone text treatment shown in the A/B comparison (E013). Also **both editorial overlay and spatial typography depending on situation** (E014).

**Working interpretation:** The text and image should feel designed together; the text does **not** need to be physically 3D to achieve this.

Two legitimate modes:

| Mode | What it is | Often useful when | Risks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Editorial overlay** | Screen-space, camera-independent text composed with subject/negative space | A phrase needs easy reading while the camera or object moves | Occlusion, arbitrary placement, overcrowded image |
| **Spatial typography** | Text intentionally integrated with scene perspective, depth or camera choreography | Physical/spatial relation contributes to the story | Distortion, poor readability, unnecessary theatricality |

**Choosing:** Prefer the simplest treatment that achieves the meaningful composition. A dedicated text-only card, large bold text or an unanimated cut can still be appropriate if the actual film calls for it; none is universally excluded.

## G01 — Technical motion graphics: integration by purpose

**Directly appreciated:** Territory Studio's *Desktop Metal* work for **how motion graphics integrate with the 3D environment and camera** (E006–E007).

**Director's explicit exception:** Integration **is not a must**; sometimes simple on-screen graphics are good enough (E007). A third valid option is **no graphics** when the visual event already communicates.

**Agent choice sequence:**
1. What real idea must the viewer learn or feel?
2. Does anchoring information in 3D genuinely clarify that physical relationship?
3. Would a clean on-screen diagram/text be easier to understand?
4. Can the camera/object communicate it without any graphic?
5. Does the treatment remain legible and mechanically truthful in a moving preview?

**Example only, not approved ECCO art direction:** A sensor-to-wall relation may benefit from spatial annotation, while a time-of-flight waveform may read better in screen space. The exact echo explanation, data conventions, geometry and client acceptability require technical review.

## Scene planning questions (not another contract)

When the shot contains words or annotations, decide and record in the **existing** [Scene Direction Contract](07-SCENE_DIRECTION_CONTRACT_v0.2.md):

- What information/emotion does the text communicate? Is the exact copy client-approved or a proposed placeholder?
- Is there a stronger wording with fewer words?
- Where is the subject and where can text breathe without concealing critical features?
- Is screen-space overlay, perspective-aware spatial type, standalone type or no type appropriate **for this shot**?
- Does it need to fade, subtly travel/settle, or simply be present? Why?
- Does it remain readable at the actual camera movement, output size and hold duration?
- Are corporate text/brand guidelines or technical measurement labels being followed?
- Which elements are **taste decisions** versus technical data needing verified units/meaning?

Typography should be authored as controllable text/graphics in an appropriate scene/compositing layer, not entrusted to generative video to invent exact wording or preserve legibility.

## Discovery still open

**Typography details unknown:** particular fonts, weights, kerning/tracking style, capitalization, alignment preferences, technical type scale, exact animation timing, palette and pairing with voice/music.

**Motion-graphics design language not yet assessed:** technical linework, annotation density, measurement appearance, graphic transitions, data animation systems, line thicknesses, chart treatment, and whether particular visual metaphors feel sophisticated or excessive. These **must not** be invented as confirmed tastes from Territory alone.

Before turning an illustrative choice into Rolladice preference, collect real still/video comparison(s), specific shot/timestamp, the director's choice and reasoning, exceptions, and approval state in the [evidence register](02-TASTE_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md). This module records **taste preference**, not client approval of any ECCO panel.
