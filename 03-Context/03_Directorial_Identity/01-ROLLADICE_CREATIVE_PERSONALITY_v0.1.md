# Rolladice Creative Personality — v0.1

**Date:** 14 October 2026  
**Status:** Working internal creative identity — **not** a complete or immutable style guide  
**Scope:** Rolladice creative/directorial taste; first applied to MIT Technologies films, reusable elsewhere  
**Evidence:** [Taste Evidence Register](02-TASTE_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md)

> **Provisional thesis:** Give things presence. Give space meaning. Let images breathe.

This sentence expresses emerging taste, not a client-approved slogan.

## 1. The difference we are after

Distinction comes from the quality of **artistic judgment**: what to reveal, where to place the camera, when to move or stop, how to use space, how much information to show and which additions make the work weaker.

A working shot is not automatically a good shot. A technically competent operator can complete the animation; a distinctive director makes every meaningful view feel authored, expressive and appropriate to its subject.

**Do not reduce this identity to a recipe** (always dark, always minimal, always slow, always diagonal). It is a set of preferences and reasoning principles that help choose among viable options.

## 2. Strongest confirmed signals

### P01 — Effortless monumentality / expressive spatial authorship

**Source:** strongly positive reaction to FutureDeluxe; [E001–E002](02-TASTE_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md).

The director admires creative use of space that gives a subject **bigger-than-life presence**. The frame itself should carry a message or emotion; even a still image can speak. Unique angles, positioning, framing and depth make images feel individual rather than templated.

Aesthetic sophistication is achieved without forced intricacy or extravagance. The image should feel as though it is **breathing, not straining**.

**Operational reading:**
- Build meaningful relationships among subject, negative space, foreground and background.
- Judge whether an intended hero frame communicates before relying on motion/effects/music to make it interesting.
- Give each scene its own spatial premise; do not copy one successful composition into every shot.
- Monumentality comes from visual relationships, not falsifying object dimensions.
- Restraint is contextual: a busy or colorful image can still breathe; an empty image can still feel forced.

**Not established:** a mandatory color palette, specific lens, preferred number of objects, minimalism as a doctrine or a fixed duration for holds.

### P02 — Refined technological presence

**Source:** positive reaction to Tendril, notably Figure F.02; [E003](02-TASTE_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md).

The director values work that feels **premium and simple**, showcases technological advancement compellingly and gives an engineered object sophistication without unnecessary extravagance.

**Operational reading:**
- Let authentic physical form, materials, detail and craftsmanship be legible.
- Use the cinematic treatment to elevate genuine technological achievement rather than obscure it.
- Treat fine material rendering and visual clarity as means to make engineering feel consequential.
- Prefer motivated visual choices over an accumulation of decorative technological effects.

**Not established:** exact metallic material, lighting rig, finishing LUT or degree of photorealism for every scene.

### P03 — Camera as guided discovery / choreographed reveal

**Source:** detailed Tendril camera preference; [E004](02-TASTE_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md).

The director particularly values how a camera **flows and navigates the object**. **Each stop is a showcase**; travel between views remains fluid and carries a **sub-narrative through timing, acceleration and rhythm**.

This is stronger and more specific than a generic preference for smooth orbiting.

**Operational reading:**
- Design camera paths as a chain of purposeful *visual arrivals*, not just start and end transforms.
- Each key arrival should communicate something: silhouette, detail, mechanism, scale, relationship or meaning.
- Travel should maintain legible spatial continuity while building anticipation or reorienting attention.
- Shape pace, acceleration, deceleration and holds as **motion phrasing**. A compelling transition is narratively active, not dead transport.
- Favor camera moves that feel aware of subject geometry and audience understanding.
- Allow deliberate cuts, still cameras or abrupt shifts where they serve a better purpose; fluidity is a preference, not a law.

**Avoid as habitual defaults:** lazy constant-speed 360° spins, aimless ornamental camera motion, stops at weak views and accelerations without a discernible visual purpose.

**Not established:** specific keyframe easing curves, camera rail topology, number of stops, focal lengths or move durations. These require scene-by-scene design and review.

## 5. Working directorial principles

These are **interpretations** of confirmed feedback, not quotations or hard stylistic requirements.

1. **A still should speak.** Check significant key compositions without sound or motion: does the arrangement already communicate presence, curiosity, meaning or emotion?
2. **Presence before spectacle.** Make the subject compelling through view, scale relationship, depth and material rather than automatically adding effects.
3. **Showcase through arrivals.** Key camera viewpoints should have a reason to exist; shape the travel between them into a continuous miniature narrative when appropriate.
4. **Complexity must earn its place.** Sophistication is not proportional to number of lights, layers, particles, cuts or graphics.
5. **Give the image room.** Every important element should have a place in a hierarchy, while acknowledging that densely packed compositions can still be excellent.
6. **Vary the expression, retain the judgment.** Consistency comes from intentional decisions, not repeating identical framing, environment or speed.
7. **Creative surprise is welcome.** The director may depart from an established preference when an alternative clearly serves the scene better.

## 6. How this personality combines with MIT's identity

This is **Rolladice's** directorial taste, not MIT's official brand. It must be adapted through [MIT Cinematic Production Identity](../01_Foundation/03-MIT_Cinematic_Production_Identity_v0.1.md) and the [technical baseline](../01_Foundation/02-ENGINEERING_BASELINE.md).

For MIT's engineering films:
- **Accuracy before beauty:** product geometry, sensor location, physical relationships and approved measurements cannot be changed to make the picture attractive.
- **Comprehension before showmanship:** camera/graphics should explain the technology; expensive-looking confusion is still confusion.
- **Creative freedom in the remaining space:** unusual framing, spatial drama, clean material presentation, fluid reveals and strong visual hierarchy can make the actual technology monumental.
- **Explain the invisible with causal meaning:** effects must describe a verified or explicitly illustrative process, not imply unverified physical simulation.
- **Treat logo, type, palette and technical approval as separate constraints:** do not promote this personality to an official MIT identity.

**Example application (not a locked shot):** introduce the ECCO tool with a memorable, mechanically faithful composition; travel into a sensor-region showcase using a fluid, paced camera arc; resolve on a readable mechanical relationship. A measurement graphic should appear when it helps explain a specific interaction, and remain subordinate to the engineering content. The actual shot path, sensing action and technical meaning require storyboard design and client verification.

## 7. Important anti-patterns and limitations

Do **not** assume the director wants:
- Minimalism in every circumstance, blank space for its own sake or slow movements by default.
- The same hero angle in every shot, or constant orbiting marketed as elegance.
- A specific color, lighting style, focal length or glossy-metal recipe without testing.
- Extravagant effects added to prove production value.
- A literal mix of FutureDeluxe and Tendril styles; synthesize principles into original solutions.
- A client-specific technical depiction whose meaning is guessed from aesthetics.

A visually striking image can still be rejected for narrative, technical or brand reasons. Conversely, a restrained or static technical image may be the best choice.

2. **Typography** — editorial versus environmental text; typographic scale, placement and animation behavior.
3. **Motion graphics** — visual form language, line weight, spatial anchoring, transitions and density.
4. **Lighting** — preferred use of darkness, reveal, contrast, reflection and shadow in different scenarios.
5. **Camera analysis by shot** — identify precise moments in Tendril's Figure F.02 to validate rhythm and arrival hypotheses.
6. **Editing and sound** — how cuts, sonic accents and held frames affect emotional pacing.

Do not fill these gaps by inventing preferences. Request comparisons or record actual selections.
